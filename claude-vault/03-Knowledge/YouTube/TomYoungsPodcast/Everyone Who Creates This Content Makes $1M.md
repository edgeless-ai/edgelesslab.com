# Everyone Who Creates This Content Makes $1M

**Tom Youngs** breaks down the **Repel → Dissolve → Invite** framework for creating content that pre-sells, so you go from hearing "I just need to think about it" to "Can you send me the payment link?"

## Core Framework: Repel, Dissolve, Invite

### 1. REPEL — Qualify using inside language
Use the exact dialogue running inside your perfect future client's head. Don't describe their situation from the outside — quote their internal conversation. This acts as a dog whistle: it magnetizes the right people and repels the wrong ones.

**Inside language example:** "I'm so tired of sending hundreds of DMs every day only to get told I just need to think about it."
**Outside language (don't use):** "Most coaches struggle to get leads."

### 2. DISSOLVE — Remove their fears
Address the three core objections:
- "I don't have enough time"
- "I need to think about it" (fear they won't get the result)
- "I need to speak to my partner"

All objections stem from one root: they don't believe you can get them the result. Show evidence and remove the fear.

### 3. INVITE — Sell from the back foot
Frame it as an invitation, not a conviction. "If that sounds like you, here's the link." The reader chooses to buy — you don't convince them.

## Key Concepts

- **Pre-sold post**: A piece of content that pre-sells the viewer on the value of working with you before they ever see a product page
- **Micro cult messaging**: Content that creates a following of people who are all-in, ready to go, and never give you headaches
- **Seven most expensive words**: "I just need to think about it"
- **Seven most lucrative words**: "Can you send me the payment link?"
- **Your word is your whistle**: The words you use vibrate at a frequency only your perfect future client can hear
- **Energy vampires**: Clients who aren't all-in, dip their toes, cancel early. Wrong messaging attracts them.
- **The "cancel anytime" trap**: One small phrase change in your offer can completely change the makeup of your client base for months
