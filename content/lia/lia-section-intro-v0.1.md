## What is LIA?

Latent Information Archaeology is an Edgeless Lab research program about the difference between an artifact's content and the way that content takes form. Its founding question is practical: what can choices of spelling, layout, notation, formatting, or structure tell us about the circumstances of production—and what can they not tell us?

LIA proposes a shared vocabulary for comparing such questions without erasing the differences between clay tablets, printed pages, and digital files. A pattern might reflect a tool, a habit, a convention, a constraint, or a later transformation. Calling it information does not establish intention, and successful prediction does not by itself identify who made a choice or why.

This section begins with a methodological field note and two interactive creative demos. The note reads published precedents and explains the proposed discipline: name the representation, separate observation from explanation, check attribution and selection, and preserve “unidentified” as an honest outcome. The demos are exploratory interfaces, not empirical confirmation of the research framework. One concerns the software artist LIA: that shared name is not an identification of the artist with this research program.

The program's proposed value is something to test against existing approaches. This is a place to inspect questions and instruments in development, not an announcement that a hidden message or a new historical mechanism has been discovered.
